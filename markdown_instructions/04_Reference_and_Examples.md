# Submission checklist

Run all checks before you lock in a PASS / FAIL verdictIt's the single biggest defense against vibe grading.Did the model open the right files?A model that ignored the workspace and answered from world knowledge alone is almost certainly failing.Does the math hold up?Pull the key numbers out of each response and verify them against the files. Don't trust the response's own arithmetic.Did the model respect every explicit constraint?Walk through each constraint you stated in the prompt and check whether the response complied. Each violated constraint is a discrete failure, not a vibe.Did the model surface your hidden finding?Usually the make-or-break question. A model that missed the headline finding is failing substantively. A model that caught it should get credit for that, even if other parts are rough.Is the format right?Headings, dollar formatting, tables - did the model deliver in the shape you asked for? Distinguish "format defeats the purpose" (major) from "cosmetic" (minor).The biggest positive-weight criterion IS the hidden headline findingCritical, +60 to +100.The biggest negative-weight criterion is a real-world catastrophic failureHallucinated rule, regulated-advice overreach, or directly violated user constraint. Deal-breaker, -60 to -100.Justifications are forensic, not hand-wavySpecific outputs quoted, specific failures named, downstream impact explained.The rubric reads like a checklist a careful reviewer would useAtomic, weighted with discipline, includes negative coverage.Before you call a fail, sanity-check the verdictAsk yourself - would a careful reviewer actually refuse to ship this answer to the user over what I'm flagging? If "no," the issue is minor and the model probably passed. If "yes," name the exact failure - that's your justification material.

Task ID: ce08d55b-2a1b-46f6-afd1-86bbaef5d7dd

Persona
Senior software engineer at a publicly-traded tech company in San Francisco, 31, single, renter. California resident.

Prompt
so I just filed my 2025 taxes and got totally blindsided, I ended up owing $22,795 in April on top of everything that was already getting withheld from my paychecks all year. That really stung and I do not want a surprise like that again. I'm a software engineer here in San Francisco, single, no dependents, and I max out my 401k every year. A decent chunk of my pay is RSUs that vest every quarter and I sell them as soon as they vest. I have a feeling the RSUs are part of why I keep coming up short, but honestly I don't really understand the mechanics of it. What I'm trying to figure out is this: looking at my 2026 pay stubs so far and comparing them to what last year actually looked like, am I on track to owe again for 2026, and if so roughly how much should I be setting aside now so I'm not caught off guard in April? My federal withholding looks like it's running about $5,000 a month right now. I'd really like an actual dollar figure I can plan around, not an "it depends" answer, and I want to understand why this keeps happening to me so I can actually stop it instead of just bracing for it every spring. A couple of things please. Do not tell me to go hire a CPA or to buy some tax software, I am trying to actually get my own arms around these numbers myself. And please do not give me a vague range, I want you to commit to a number. Lay it out for me in these sections: "What Happened in 2025," "My 2026 Outlook," and "What I Should Do," and use whole dollar amounts throughout.

A careful reviewer would refuse to ship this because the user asked a question that was fully answerable from the available files, yet was told it could not be answered, and left with no committed figure, no actionable plan, and a list of inputs to go gather themselves.
