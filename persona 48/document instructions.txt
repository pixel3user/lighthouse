This is a brilliant strategy for creating a robust evaluation task. Large Language Models (LLMs) suffer heavily from **"knowledge retrieval bias"**—when they see a document they are familiar with (like a W-2, 1040, or 1098-E), they often stop reading the actual file and instead hallucinate the standard boilerplate instructions from their training data. 

By targeting the unique, customized, or abbreviated instructions in these specific PDFs, we can easily catch models that are "cheating" rather than performing true document extraction.

Here are the most highly specific, mutually exclusive instructions I found in these documents that differ from the standard templates:

### 1. The W-2 "Box 12" Trap (`W2_Crestline_2025.pdf`)
* **The Reality:** Standard IRS W-2s have a massive "Box 12 Codes" reference section on the back that lists every code from A to ZZ. 
* **The Unique Document Text:** On page 6 of Winifred's W-2, under the specific heading **"Box 12 Code Reference"**, the document *only* lists two codes: 
  * `Code A. Uncollected Social Security or RRTA tax on tips.`
  * `Code B. Uncollected Medicare tax on tips.`
* **The Trick:** If you ask the model, *"According strictly to the 'Box 12 Code Reference' list on page 6 of my W-2, what does Code D stand for?"*, the correct answer is that **it is not listed in that section**. An LLM relying on its training data will fail by proudly telling you it stands for 401(k) elective deferrals, proving it didn't read the actual section.

### 2. The 1040 "Custom Tables" Trap (`form1040_2024_prior_year_return.pdf`)
* **The Reality:** The IRS Form 1040 instructions are standardized.
* **The Unique Document Text:** The software that generated this 1040 injected highly customized tables on pages 5 and 6 that do not exist on standard IRS forms:
  * A **"Three-Year Federal Tax Comparison"** table comparing her 2022, 2023, and 2024 income, adjustments, and refunds.
  * An **"IRS Interest Rates on Underpayments and Overpayments — Recent Quarters"** table showing a flat 8% rate for all quarters in 2024 and 2025.
* **The Trick:** Ask the model to extract the "2022 Adjusted gross income" from the Three-Year Comparison table, or the "Q3 2025 Individual underpayment rate". Models that assume this is just a standard 1040 will fail to find these custom software-generated tables.

### 3. The 401(k) Fee & Match Trap (`retirement_401k_statement_Northmark_2026_Q2.pdf`)
* **The Reality:** 401(k) statements vary wildly by provider.
* **The Unique Document Text:** 
  * Under *Fee Disclosure*: "Recordkeeping and administrative fees for this period were **$9.50**... Investment expense ratios range from **0.03% to 0.34%** annually depending on the fund."
  * Under *Contribution Detail*: "Employer matching contributions are **50% of the first 4%** of eligible pay you defer."
* **The Trick:** Ask the model to calculate exactly how much her employer would match if she deferred $10,000, or ask for the exact range of her investment expense ratios. Models cannot guess these numbers; they must actively read the fine print.

### 4. The Bank Statement Funds Availability Trap (`bank_checking_statement_2026_Q2.pdf`)
* **The Reality:** For years, standard bank hold disclosures (Reg CC) stated that the first $200 of a check was available the next day. The law recently changed this to $225. 
* **The Unique Document Text:** Page 4 under *Funds Availability* explicitly states: "...the first **$225** of a day's check deposits are generally available the first business day after the deposit."
* **The Trick:** Ask the model, *"According to my bank statement's Funds Availability policy, what is the exact dollar amount of a check deposit that is generally available on the first business day?"* Models relying on older training data will confidently hallucinate "$200".

### 5. The 1098-E Truncated Instructions (`form1098E_StudentLoanInterest_2025.pdf`)
* **The Reality:** The official IRS 1098-E contains instructions for both the Student and the Borrower, and includes a specific URL (`irs.gov/Form1098E`).
* **The Unique Document Text:** Page 2 of this PDF is a highly truncated, custom version of the instructions. It only includes "Instructions for Borrower", and the IRS URL is entirely missing.
* **The Trick:** Ask the model, *"What website URL does my 1098-E document tell me to visit for more information?"* A model that is hallucinating will spit out the standard `irs.gov/Form1098E` link, whereas the correct answer is that no URL is provided in the document's instructions.
