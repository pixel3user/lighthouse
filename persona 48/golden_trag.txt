TRAJECTORY

THREAD 1 — How much of the check can actually be paid, and Meridian's balance after it

1. Open bank_checking_statement_2026_Q2.pdf and go to the Funds Availability (continued)
   section on page 4. Read the sentence "Funds from cash, wire transfers, and the first $225
   of a day's check deposits are generally available the first business day after the
   deposit." Retrieve the next-business-day check availability limit of $225.00.

2. Calculate the amount usable tomorrow using the $500.00 check from the prompt and the
   limit from Step 1: only $225.00 of the check is available, and $275.00 is held. The
   held $275.00 has no stated release date in the statement, so it is excluded from every
   figure below.

3. Open credit_card_statement_MeridianVisa_2026_06.pdf page 1, Account Summary, and
   retrieve previous balance $5,133.45, payments and credits ($104.80), purchases and
   debits $89.13, interest charged $122.22, and new balance $5,240.00. From Payment
   Information retrieve minimum payment due $104.80 and payment due date July 17, 2026.
   From the APR paragraph retrieve the purchase APR of 27.99%.

4. Calculate Meridian's principal immediately after the one-time payment using Step 2 and
   Step 3: $5,240.00 - $225.00 = $5,015.00. This is the answer to the first question and
   the starting balance for both scenarios.

THREAD 2 — Which account should get the recurring extra money

5. Open credit_card_statement_SummitMC_2026_06.pdf page 1 and retrieve new balance
   $3,480.00, APR 22.99%, minimum payment due $69.60, and interest charged $66.67.

6. Open personal_loan_statement_LibertyPoint_2026_06.pdf page 1 and retrieve current
   principal balance $7,850.00, fixed APR 14.99%, and scheduled monthly payment $278.26.

7. Open student_loan_statement_Sterling_2026_06.pdf page 1 and retrieve current principal
   balance $31,430.00, fixed interest rate 8.75%, and scheduled monthly payment $342.00.

8. Open debt_payoff_tracker_DRAFT_2026.xlsx and go to the Payoff order tab. Read the order
   1 Summit, 2 Meridian, 3 Liberty Point, 4 Sterling, with the stated reasons referring to
   smallest balance and quick win, and read the bottom row "Paying extra on now: Nuisance
   (Summit Rewards Mastercard)." Conclude the tracker is ordered by balance size, i.e. a
   snowball, and that Summit currently receives the extra.

9. Sort the four debts by APR descending using Steps 3, 5, 6 and 7: Meridian 27.99%,
   Summit 22.99%, Liberty Point 14.99%, Sterling 8.75%. Because the prompt's objective is
   least total interest, the recurring extra belongs on Meridian, not Summit. This answers
   the second question.

THREAD 3 — The hidden problem with Meridian's regular payment

10. Calculate the gap between Meridian's regular payment and its monthly interest using
    Step 3: $104.80 - $122.22 = -$17.42. The regular payment is $17.42 short of one
    month's interest, so the balance climbs even when the payment is made on time.

11. Confirm this on credit_card_statement_MeridianVisa_2026_06.pdf page 2, "Minimum Payment
    and Payoff Scenarios." Read the Months to pay off column: it reads "never" for every
    fixed monthly payment of $107.00, $112.00, $117.00 and $122.00. Note for accuracy that
    June's $106.55 balance increase ($5,133.45 to $5,240.00) also includes $89.13 of
    purchases from Step 3, so the payment shortfall is the durable cause, not the whole of
    that one month's rise.

12. Calculate the shortfall that survives the one-time payment, using the $5,015.00 from
    Step 4 and the monthly rate from Step 17 below (27.99% / 12 = 0.023325):
    $5,015.00 x 0.023325 = $116.97 of interest against the same $104.80 payment, a $12.17
    monthly shortfall. With no new card charges, Meridian still grows. This is the hidden
    finding the prompt never mentions.

THREAD 4 — The real monthly leftover

13. Open paystub_Crestline_2026_06.pdf page 1 and retrieve pay frequency "Semimonthly,

    bank_checking_statement_2026_Q2.pdf) and find the payroll credits. There are six at
    $1,976.33 each, dated 04/15, 04/30, 05/15, 05/29, 06/15 and 06/30, confirming two per

15. Open debt_payoff_tracker_DRAFT_2026.xlsx and go to the Budget tab. Retrieve take-home

    food/dining/gas $455.00, subscriptions $55.46, "Minimums on the other debts" $490.00
    with the column C note "the ones I'm not attacking", and leftover $2,970.20.
    Calculate the income error using Step 13 and Step 15: $5,466.66 / 2 = $2,733.33, which
    is the GROSS figure from Step 13, so the line is gross pay mislabelled as take-home.
    Calculate the overstatement: $5,466.66 - $3,952.66 = $1,514.00 per month, which
    reconciles exactly to ($229.33 + $527.67) x 2 = $1,514.00.

16. Identify the three debts covered by the $490.00 line. From Step 8 the extra currently
    goes to Summit, so the ones she is not attacking are Meridian, Liberty Point and
    Sterling. Calculate their true total using Steps 3, 6 and 7: $104.80 + $278.26 +
    $342.00 = $725.06, so the line is understated by $725.06 - $490.00 = $235.06. Confirm
    the scope by reproducing her own formula from the leftover row's note "take-home minus
    everything above": 5466.66 - 1250 - 246 - 455 - 55.46 - 490 = 2970.20, which matches
    her leftover cell exactly. Netting all four minimums instead gives $2,900.60 and does
    not reconcile, so Summit's $69.60 is not part of this line and must not be subtracted
    again.

17. Calculate the real monthly leftover using Step 14 and Step 16, leaving the rent,
    utilities, food and subscription lines exactly as recorded per the prompt's
    instruction: $3,952.66 - $1,250.00 - $246.00 - $455.00 - $55.46 - $725.06 = $1,221.14.
    Compare with her $2,970.20: she has been budgeting around $1,514.00 + $235.06 =
    $1,749.06 a month that does not exist. The one-time check payment is excluded from this
    recurring figure per the prompt.

THREAD 5 — Scenario A, regular June payments continued July through December

    BANK VISA PMT AUTOPAY $104.80 on 06/17, SUMMIT REWARDS MC AUTOPAY $615.20 on 06/18,
    LIBERTY POINT LENDING LOAN PMT $278.26 on 06/20, and STERLING STUDENT LOAN SVCG PMT
    $342.00 on 06/22. Calculate the regular monthly debt outflow: $104.80 + $615.20 +

    / 12, rounded to cents, added to the balance before the payment is subtracted. This is
    the issuers' own method.

FINAL ANSWER

31. Only $225.00 of the $500.00 check is available tomorrow, so the one-time payment leaves
    the Meridian Visa at $5,015.00. The recurring extra of $545.60 should move from Summit
    to Meridian starting with the payment due July 17, 2026, because Meridian's 27.99% APR
    is the highest and its $104.80 regular payment is below its monthly interest, so that
    balance grows on its own. The real monthly leftover is $1,221.14, not the $2,970.20 on
    the Budget tab, which is overstated by $1,749.06, and after the one-time $9.50 401(k)
    recordkeeping fee the adjusted leftover is $1,211.64. Changing nothing else leaves
    Meridian at $5,092.44, Summit at $27.10, Liberty Point at $6,734.47 and Sterling at
    $30,740.60 after the December regular payment, a total of $42,594.61. Redirecting the
    extra to Meridian for the same $1,340.26 monthly outflow leaves Meridian at $1,621.90,
    Summit at $3,461.56, Liberty Point at $6,734.47 and Sterling at $30,740.60, a total of
    $42,558.53. The difference is $36.08 less total debt under the redirected plan.
